2026-04-22 21:45 terrain-shape · Phase 2b — pile accumulates in front of blade 🏔️

Your feedback: "Can the sand accumulate in front of the blade as it's pushing?" ✓

The fix — scrape and deposit zones now form a conveyor belt:

  • Scrape zone sits AT the blade edge. Cells directly under the blade's cutting line get shaved to the cutting floor.
  • Deposit zone sits 1 cell IN FRONT. All shaved dirt lands into a tight, 1-cell-deep band right ahead of the blade.
  • Non-overlapping. As the dozer drives forward, cells that were deposit-cells last tick become scrape-cells this tick — so the pile rolls forward with the blade, picking up new terrain along the way. The longer you push, the more the pile grows.
  • Narrower deposit swath. Dirt concentrates into fewer cells, so the pile reads as a distinct ridge instead of a smeared bump.

Watch the mid-scene mound form around t=5s — that's entirely the dozer's work. When the dozer turns and drives away, the pile persists.

2026-04-22 17:00 terrain-shape · Phase 1c — tilt redesign (visual-only, no physics fight) 🛻

Your feedback: "We went too far. Turning isn't smooth anymore and driving feels weird. Less aggressive pitching." ✓

The Phase 1b problem (now reverted): I was slerping the entire physics body basis each frame toward the terrain normal. That meant yaw (your turn input) was also being smoothed — every frame, the tilt lerp was dragging the yaw back toward a previous sample. On top of that, the tilted collider intersected the ground, so I had to hard-snap Y, which replaced gravity and made the motion feel synthetic.

The redesign — separate physics from visual:

  • Body stays upright. The CharacterBody3D + box collider rotate only on Y (yaw). Gravity + is_on_floor + move_and_slide work exactly like before any tilt existed. Turning is back to instant/smooth.
  • Tilt lives on a child visual node. All the mesh boxes are parented to a Node3D child instead of the body directly. That child gets a pitch-and-roll-only basis each frame.
  • Smooth the normal, not the basis. The terrain normal is lerped at 6/sec before being converted into a tilt basis. Because smoothing is on the input (the normal) and not on the output (the body rotation), it can't interfere with yaw.
  • No more Y-snap hack. Body bottom rests on the heightmap via physics. No corners-into-ground issue because the collider is never tilted.

Net result: controls feel like flat-ground driving, but the dozer visibly leans into slopes. Visual tilt is subtle by design — readable without being bouncy.

Review

  1. Turning feel good now?
  2. Tilt visible enough, or too subtle? (Dial via NORMAL_LERP, currently 6/sec.)
  3. Ready for Phase 2 (blade deforms terrain) or park this?
