2026-04-22 10:30 NEW EXPERIMENT · backhoe-scoop · Phase 1 scoop mechanic

New experiment #2: experiments/backhoe-scoop/. Stationary backhoe with a 3-DOF articulated arm and a hollow AnimatableBody3D bucket. 120 small sand balls (radius 0.08, mass 0.055) — chunky gravel scale. Validates the scoop mechanic before adding a truck in Phase 2.

Controls (all keyboard):

  • A / D or ← / →: swing arm left/right (aim)
  • W / S or ↑ / ↓: raise / lower arm (dig)
  • Space (hold): tip bucket forward to dump; release to level

The bucket auto-stays-level as you raise and lower the arm (so scooped balls don't fall out) — space-bar overrides that to pour. Open at top + front; walls on back + sides + floor + low front lip so balls stay in during transport.

To try it: experiments/backhoe-scoop/project.godot. Lower the bucket into the pile, lift, swing to aim, hold Space to pour.

The real question (answer after playing)

  1. Is the scoop mechanic satisfying on its own — just moving sand around?
  2. Does the bucket pick up the right amount of sand per scoop, or does it feel too empty / too full?
  3. Does the 3-key control scheme (swing / pitch / tip) feel intuitive or confusing?

If fun → Phase 2 adds a parked truck bed as drop target. Reply: /dailies-feedback [notes]

2026-04-22 10:00 sand-push · Phase 1b · push fixes + tracks + edge walls

Four fixes shipped (Medium, ~35 min):

  1. Root-cause bug found: the dozer's collision box was 35 cm above the ground, so it literally flew over the balls instead of touching them. Dropped the body to y=0; collider now covers y=[0, 0.9] — hits every sphere. PUSH_SCALE also tuned: speed × 0.45 × impulse instead of speed × 1.2 × 0.1. Should feel like a real shove.
  2. Visible tracks behind the dozer. Every 0.12 sec while moving, a dark dirt rectangle is dropped under each track. Fades to transparent over 4.5 sec. Shows up clearly on the sandy ground.
  3. Edge walls — invisible StaticBody3D around the 16×16 playfield on collision layer 2. Dozer includes layer 2 in its collision mask → stops at the edge. Sand is on layer 1 only → sand passes through the walls and falls off the canvas. Shoved balls that fall below y=−3 are auto-deleted.
  4. Same keyboard controls: WASD or arrow keys.

Can't demo push/tracks/falling in a no-input MP4. Run locally and shove the pile toward an edge — balls should fall off, dozer should stop at the edge.

Review questions

  1. Is pushing the sand with the dozer satisfying now?
  2. Do visible tracks fade at a good rate (too fast / too slow)?
  3. Falling-off-the-edge — fun, or does it feel empty when balls disappear?
